# Analysis fields and rules

## Video card

For each video record:

- title promise and thumbnail expression
- first-30-second hook
- central conflict
- information release order
- claim presentation method
- evidence use
- emotional curve
- sentence length and spoken-language level
- transitions, suspense, risk disclosure, and CTA
- transferable high-level structures
- elements that must not be copied

Use `OBSERVED`, `INFERRED`, and `UNAVAILABLE` evidence labels per field. If no transcript or direct viewing is available, set hook, sentence rhythm, and exact CTA to `UNAVAILABLE`; do not infer them from a title.

## Channel profile

Aggregate recurring hook functions, chapter sequences, evidence order, pacing, uncertainty habits, CTA intensity, and anti-patterns. Three videos are below the research-design target, so pilot profiles must remain `PROVISIONAL`.

## Cross-creator comparison

Compare high-level dimensions only: promise specificity, time-to-thesis, evidence-before-opinion vs opinion-before-evidence, pacing, macro-to-micro direction, explanation density, uncertainty, and CTA. List the source video IDs for each conclusion.

## Originality

Normalize whitespace and punctuation for checks, then report:

- longest contiguous matching span
- character n-gram Jaccard overlap
- repeated phrase locations
- cross-draft similarity

Heuristic thresholds are QA signals, not legal safe harbors. Block unresolved long overlaps, source-specific phrases, signature openings, unique metaphors, personal stories, or persona imitation.

## Finance wording

Use calibrated phrasing: “官方資料顯示” for direct facts, “這可能意味著” for inference, and “仍需觀察” for uncertainty. Never turn a scenario into a forecast or a market observation into a recommendation.
